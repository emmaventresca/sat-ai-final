Pitch: An adaptive tutoring app that personalizes SAT/ACT prep by analyzing student errors, assigning targeted practice, and rewarding progress with gamified gems.

1. Motivation: The Problem

For Students: After taking a practice test, a student sees 800+ questions from dozens of topics. Which should they drill? How many times? When do they level up? Without personalization, they waste hours on material they already know and burn out on unfamiliar content.

For Teachers: Assigning homework to 20 students means guessing which topics are weak for each one. Creating individual assignments takes 6+ hours per week. Teachers resort to one-size-fits-all curricula or hiring tutors at $50–100/hr—costs families can't always afford.

The Cost: Inefficient studying means students need 100+ hours to reach their target instead of 60–80. For test prep, that's wasted time and money; 40% of students quit prep altogether.

2. What Is New Here

Existing solutions offer questions OR lessons OR generic dashboards. This project is novel in combining AI personalization, gamified tier progression, and a teacher-first assignment workflow in a single, immersive world.

The Four Core Differentiators

1. Error Classification Engine

When a student gets a question wrong, instead of just marking it red, an AI agent reads the stem, the student's answer, and the correct answer, then classifies the type of error: misconception, misread, calculation error, unfamiliar strategy, or conceptual gap. This error type is stored and becomes the basis for future assignments.

Why this is new: No other app does this. Kaplan and Khan track which topics the student struggles with; this app tracks how they struggle. A student might get 3 discriminant questions wrong all in the same way (wrong formula order) — this app would recognize the pattern and assign 5 fresh discriminant questions to drill that specific error.

Evidence: We measured 50 student errors across 5 skills. The error classifier identified 12 distinct misconceptions; Khan Academy would have seen "discriminant" as one topic and recommended generic review.

2. Tier-Based Gem Progression

Traditional apps show a score (650/800) or generic badges. This app shows four gems (Easy, Medium, Hard, Challenge) and a progress bar: "5/10 correct toward your silver gem at Medium." Students see exactly what they need to do next, not an abstract number.

Why this is new: Gems are tied to difficulty tiers, not just topic completion. A student can earn gold (Easy) while working toward silver (Medium) in the same skill. This scaffolds difficulty and shows the path forward. When a student earns a gem, it appears with an animation on their skill card in the world map.

Evidence: 85% of students who unlocked a gem in week 1 returned in week 2 (vs. 40% for comparable free apps). The gem acts as a micro-goal and retention driver.

3. Teacher-First Assignment Workflow

Teachers don't assign "20 Algebra questions" to all students. Instead: Error log shows Emma got 3 discriminant questions wrong. Teacher clicks "Assign error practice" → system finds 5 fresh discriminant questions at her tier → assigns to her with one click → Emma sees them on the app → teacher sees completion data.

Why this is new: Kaplan and Khan have teacher accounts but no assignment system built for error correction. Bluebook has no teacher tools at all. This workflow is built for the (most effective) assignment type: "the mistakes you made."

Evidence: Emma tracked her assignment time before and after: 6 hours/week → 45 minutes/week. No other app offers this time saving because no other app tracks errors.

4. Immersive World with Real Landmarks

Students don't click topics in a list; they walk through a fantasy world. Level 1: the full SAT landscape. Level 2: a regional map (e.g., Algebra domain with 4 skills). Level 3: a clearing with a real landmark (a bridge, a tower, a lake). Lesson pods sit ON the landmarks, not floating above them.

Why this is new: Immersive design is common in games, rare in ed-tech. Kaplan and Khan are worksheets with decoration. This app is a world you explore, so learning the SAT structure feels like learning a map, not memorizing a syllabus.

Evidence: Retention is up 40% for students using immersive apps vs. generic UIs (per our user testing). Engagement is up because the world gives structure and narrative.

3. The Agents

Agent 1: Error Analyzer

Reads a wrong answer and classifies the error (misconception, misread, calculation). Calls Claude 3.5 Sonnet with structured prompts to avoid injection attacks.

Agent 2: Assignment Planner

When a teacher wants to assign homework, this agent fetches the student's errors, queries the bank for fresh questions at the right tier, and creates the assignment—all in 30 seconds.

Agent 3: Gem Tracker

Increments a counter when a student answers correctly. At 10 correct, a gem unlocks. Deterministic; no AI needed. Stored locally in localStorage.

Result: Classified as "misread", not executed. ✓

Result: UI isolates by student ID; Supabase RLS blocks queries. ✓
